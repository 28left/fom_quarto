1. Hook (0:00–0:03)

NoteSay

A foundation should formalize its own metatheory — that was our “foundational loop.” Today: the price of that. Because \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) can talk about its own proofs, it cannot certify its own consistency. That failure gives us a way to compare theories.

  • Point back to the “\(2 + 8 = 10\) and its formal shadow” example: \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) contains copies of its own syntax.
ImportantBoard: guiding question

Can we prove that \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) + “there is an inaccessible” is consistent, assuming \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) is?

2. Gödel’s incompleteness theorems (0:03–0:18)

Goal: Students leave knowing \(\operatorname{Con}(T)\) is a concrete arithmetic sentence and that \({}T\) cannot prove it.

2a. Setup (3 min)

Hypotheses on a theory \({}T\):

  1. \({}T\) is consistent.
  2. \({}T\) is recursively axiomatized: the set of axioms is decidable. \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) qualifies despite its axiom schemes.
  3. \({}T\) interprets a modest amount of arithmetic. Robinson’s \(\mathsf{Q}\) suffices; \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) does, via \({}\omega\).

2b. Arithmetization (4 min)

  • Formulas and proofs are finite strings \(\to\) code them as natural numbers (or hereditarily finite sets in \(\mathsf{ZFC}\)).
  • “\(p\) codes a \(T\)-proof of \(\varphi\)” is decidable, hence expressible by a \(\Delta_0\) arithmetic formula.
  • So \(\operatorname{Prov}_T(x) \equiv \exists p\,(p \text{ codes a proof of } x)\) is \(\Sigma_1\), and

\[ \operatorname{Con}(T) \;:\equiv\; \neg\,\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner 0 = 1 \urcorner). \]

ImportantBoard emphasis

\(\operatorname{Con}(T)\) is \(\Pi^0_1\) — “no natural number codes a proof of a contradiction.” It is a statement about \(\mathbb{N}\), nothing more.

2c. Diagonal lemma and G1 (4 min)

Lemma 1 (Diagonal lemma)
For every formula \(\psi(x)\) there is a sentence \({}\sigma\) with \[ T \vdash \sigma \leftrightarrow \psi(\ulcorner \sigma \urcorner). \]

Apply Lemma 1 to \(\neg\operatorname{Prov}_T\) to get \({}G\) with \[ T \vdash G \leftrightarrow \neg\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner) \qquad \text{("I am not provable").} \]

Theorem 1 (Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem)
If \({}T\) satisfies the hypotheses in 2a, then \(T \nvdash G\). If \({}T\) is moreover \(\omega\)-consistent, then \(T \nvdash \neg G\).

NoteProof

Proof. If \(T \vdash G\), then \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)\) (a proof exists and \({}T\) verifies it), and \(T \vdash \neg\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)\) via \({}G\). So \({}T\) is inconsistent.

For \(T \nvdash \neg G\): mention \(\omega\)-consistency, or Rosser’s trick for plain consistency. Don’t prove.

  • \({}G\) is true in \(\mathbb{N}\) if \({}T\) is consistent: it says exactly what we just showed.

2d. G2 (4 min)

The argument “if \({}T\) is consistent then \(T \nvdash G\)” is finitistic, so it can be carried out inside \({}T\): \[ T \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T) \to G. \]

Theorem 2 (Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem)
If \({}T\) is consistent, then \(T \nvdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\).

NoteProof

Proof. If \(T \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\), then \(T \vdash G\), contradicting Theorem 1.

  • “Formalizable” = the Hilbert–Bernays derivability conditions. Name them; don’t prove them.
  • Takeaway for Hilbert’s program: finitistic metamathematics is formalizable in \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), so it cannot establish the consistency of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\).

2e. Bridge to set theory (1 min, while writing)

In \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), by soundness and completeness: \[ \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \iff \exists M\, (M \models \mathsf{ZFC}). \]

So \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) cannot prove there is a set model of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) — let alone a transitive one like \(V_\kappa\).

3. Inaccessibles revisited (0:18–0:25)

Goal: The key logical move of the lecture. Write Theorem 3 and its proof on the board in full.

3a. Recall and first consequence (2 min)

From the notes: if \({}\kappa\) is inaccessible, then \(V_\kappa \models \mathsf{ZFC}\). Therefore \[ \mathsf{ZFC}+ \exists\,\mathsf{IC} \;\vdash\; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}). \]

By Theorem 2: if \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) is consistent, then \(\mathsf{ZFC}\nvdash \exists\,\mathsf{IC}\).

3b. Contrast with the absoluteness argument (3 min)

The notes’ argument (least inaccessible \({}\kappa\), then \(V_\kappa \models \neg\exists\,\mathsf{IC}\)) shows only \[ \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{IC}) \quad \Rightarrow \quad \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\neg\mathsf{IC}). \] Theorem 2 gives something much stronger:

Theorem 3 (Key Claim)
If \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) is consistent, then \[ \mathsf{ZFC}\nvdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \to \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}). \]

NoteProof

Proof. Suppose \(\mathsf{ZFC}\vdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \to \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC})\).

  1. \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\), via \(V_\kappa\).
  2. Combining, \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC})\).
  3. By Theorem 2, \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) is inconsistent.
NoteSay

We cannot even prove the relative consistency of an inaccessible. Its existence is not just unprovable — it is genuinely stronger.

3c. Pause: two kinds of independence (2 min)

\(\mathsf{CH}\) behaves completely differently: \[ \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \iff \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{CH}) \iff \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\neg\mathsf{CH}) \] (via \({}L\) and via forcing — previewed for Week 5).

  • Equiconsistent independence: \(\mathsf{CH}\). Adding it or its negation costs nothing.
  • Strength-increasing independence: \(\mathsf{IC}\). Adding it proves new arithmetic facts.

4. Consistency strength (0:25–0:35)

Goal: Make “stronger theory” a precise ordering, and show how large cardinals calibrate it.

4a. Definition (3 min)

Definition 1 (Consistency strength)
Fix a weak base theory \({}B\) (\(\mathsf{PA}\), or \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) for set-theoretic purposes). For theories \({}T, S\), \[ T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S \;:\iff\; B \vdash \operatorname{Con}(S) \to \operatorname{Con}(T). \]

  • \(T \equiv_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) if both \(T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) and \(S \le_{\operatorname{Con}} T\).
  • \(T <_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) if \(T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) and additionally \(S \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\).

By Theorem 2, \(T <_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) is a strict inequality whenever \({}S\) is consistent.

4b. Examples (4 min)

Relation Method Who
\(\mathsf{ZF}\equiv \mathsf{ZFC}\equiv \mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{GCH}\) inner model \({}L\) Gödel
\(\mathsf{ZFC}\equiv \mathsf{ZFC}+\neg\mathsf{CH}\) forcing Cohen
\(\mathsf{PA} < \mathsf{ZFC}\) \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) proves \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{PA})\) via \({}\omega\) as a model —
\(\mathsf{ZFC}< \mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{IC}\) \(V_\kappa\) plus Theorem 3 —
\(\mathsf{ZF}+\mathsf{DC}+\) “all sets of reals are Lebesgue measurable” \(\equiv \mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{IC}\) forcing (Lévy collapse) / inner model Solovay, Shelah

The last row ties back to the measure problem from the notes.

4c. Two ways to measure strength (3 min)

  1. Upper bounds — build a model. Examples: \(V_\kappa\), forcing extensions.
  2. Lower bounds — build an inner model with the large cardinal. Example: if \(\omega_1\) is inaccessible to the reals (in the relevant setting), then \(\omega_1\) is inaccessible in \({}L\).
NoteSay

Empirically, natural theories line up linearly under \(\le_{\operatorname{Con}}\), with large cardinals as the scale. Nobody knows why.

5. Weakly compact cardinals (0:35–0:45)

Goal: One more rung on the ladder, strictly above inaccessibles.

5a. Definition (1 min)

Definition 2
An uncountable cardinal \(\kappa > \omega\) is weakly compact if \[ \kappa \to (\kappa)^2_2, \] that is, every 2-coloring of \([\kappa]^2\) has a homogeneous set of size \({}\kappa\).

This is the uncountable analogue of Ramsey’s theorem \(\aleph_0 \to (\aleph_0)^2_2\).

5b. Weakly compact \(\Rightarrow\) inaccessible (5 min)

Proposition 1
Every weakly compact cardinal is inaccessible.

NoteProof

Proof. Regular (prove in class, 2 min). Suppose \(\kappa = \bigcup_{\xi<\lambda} B_\xi\) with \(\lambda < \kappa\) and each \(|B_\xi| < \kappa\). Color \(\{\alpha, \beta\}\) by “same block” vs “different blocks.”

  • Homogeneous in “same”: lies inside one block, so size \(< \kappa\).
  • Homogeneous in “different”: at most one point per block, so size \(\leq \lambda < \kappa\).

So there is no homogeneous set of size \({}\kappa\).

Strong limit (sketch, 3 min). Suppose \(2^\lambda \geq \kappa\) for some \(\lambda < \kappa\). Pick distinct \(f_\alpha \in 2^\lambda\) for \(\alpha < \kappa\).

  • Color \(\{\alpha < \beta\}\) red if \(f_\alpha <_{\mathop{\mathrm{lex}}} f_\beta\), blue otherwise.
  • A homogeneous set of size \(\kappa \geq \lambda^+\) gives a \(<_{\mathop{\mathrm{lex}}}\)-monotone sequence of length \(\lambda^+\) in \(2^\lambda\).
  • Sierpiński: \((2^\lambda, <_{\mathop{\mathrm{lex}}})\) has no increasing or decreasing sequence of length \(\lambda^+\). Contradiction.

Details are the exercise in the notes: \(2^\lambda \nrightarrow (\lambda^+)^2_2\).

5c. Why the name: equivalent forms (2 min)

Theorem 4
For inaccessible \({}\kappa\), the following are equivalent:

  1. \({}\kappa\) is weakly compact.
  2. Tree property: every \(\kappa\)-tree has a branch of length \({}\kappa\).
  3. Compactness: the infinitary logic \(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa,\kappa}\) satisfies compactness for theories of size \({}\kappa\) — hence the name.
  4. \(\Pi^1_1\)-indescribability: every \(\Pi^1_1\) property true of \((V_\kappa, \in, A)\) already holds of some \((V_\alpha, \in, A \cap V_\alpha)\) with \(\alpha < \kappa\).

State without proof.

5d. Strictly stronger than inaccessible (2 min)

  • “\({}\kappa\) is inaccessible” is \(\Pi^1_1\) over \(V_\kappa\) (regularity quantifies over subsets of \({}\kappa\)).
  • By indescribability it reflects: there are inaccessibles \(\alpha < \kappa\) — in fact stationarily many.
  • In particular \({}\kappa\) is the \(\kappa\)-th inaccessible.
  • Taking an inaccessible \(\lambda < \kappa\): \(V_\kappa \models \mathsf{ZFC}+ \exists\,\mathsf{IC}\). Hence

\[ \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{WC} \;\vdash\; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}), \]

and, by the same argument as Theorem 3, \(\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{IC} <_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{WC}\).

6. Ladder and wrap-up (0:45–0:50)

ImportantBoard: the ladder

\[ \mathsf{ZFC}\;<\; \text{inaccessible} \;<\; \text{weakly compact} \;<\; \text{Ramsey} \;<\; \text{measurable} \;<\; \cdots \;<\; \text{(Kunen: no } j : V \to V) \]

  • Link to the notes: Rowbottom’s theorem gives measurable \(\Rightarrow\) Ramsey \(\Rightarrow\) weakly compact.
  • A measurable \({}\kappa\) is a limit of weakly compacts, so the rungs are genuinely separated.
  • Kunen’s inconsistency marks a ceiling: the ladder cannot go on in the naive direction forever.

Questions for discussion:

  1. Why believe \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{IC})\) when no proof is possible relative to \(\mathsf{ZFC}\)? Candidates: reflection, the iterative conception, decades without a contradiction.
  2. The ladder has concrete arithmetic content: each rung proves new \(\Pi^0_1\) theorems (consistency statements).
  3. Next time: \({}L\) and forcing as the tools behind the equiconsistencies in 4b.

Likely student questions

Note“Isn’t \(G\) true, so why can’t we just add it?”

You can; \(T + G\) is again recursively axiomatized and has its own Gödel sentence.

Note“Doesn’t the least-inaccessible argument already show \(\mathsf{ZFC}\nvdash \mathsf{IC}\)?”

It shows \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{IC}) \Rightarrow \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+\neg\mathsf{IC})\). Theorem 2 gives the stronger Theorem 3, with no assumption needed about models.

Note“Could \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) be inconsistent?”

In principle yes, and we could never rule it out from \(\mathsf{ZFC}\). That is exactly why \(\mathsf{IC}\) counts as a new axiom rather than a theorem.

Note“Why is \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\) not about sets?”

It is \(\Pi^0_1\): a claim about all natural numbers, checkable instance by instance.
