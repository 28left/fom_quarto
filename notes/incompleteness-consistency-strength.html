Gödel’s theorems, inaccessible and weakly compact cardinals, and the hierarchy of consistency strength

Incompleteness and Consistency Strength

The price of the foundational loop

We asked of a foundational theory that it be strong enough to formalize its own metatheory. \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) meets this demand: formulas, proofs, and the axioms of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) itself are finite syntactic objects, and \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) can represent and reason about them, just as it represents “\(2 + 8 = 10\)” by a formal shadow \(\ulcorner 2 \urcorner + \ulcorner 8 \urcorner = \ulcorner 10 \urcorner\).

Gödel’s incompleteness theorems show that this strength comes at a price. A theory that can talk about its own proofs cannot prove its own consistency. Rather than a defect, this turns out to be a measuring device: it lets us compare theories by how much consistency they can certify. Large cardinal axioms will serve as the scale.

Our guiding question:

TipQuestion

Can we prove that \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) + “there is an inaccessible cardinal” is consistent, assuming that \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) is consistent?

We already know that \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) does not prove the existence of an inaccessible cardinal (if \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) is consistent). The answer to the question above will be a much stronger no.

Gödel’s incompleteness theorems

Arithmetization

Throughout this section, \({}T\) is a first-order theory satisfying the following conditions.

  1. \({}T\) is recursively axiomatized: there is an algorithm that decides whether a given formula is an axiom of \({}T\).
  2. \({}T\) interprets enough arithmetic: the axioms of Robinson’s arithmetic \(\mathsf{Q}\) (a weak, finitely axiomatized fragment of Peano arithmetic \(\mathsf{PA}\)) are provable in \({}T\), possibly after translating the language of arithmetic into the language of \({}T\).

\(\mathsf{ZFC}\) satisfies both conditions. It has infinitely many axioms because of the Comprehension and Replacement schemes, but it is decidable whether a formula is an instance of one of them. Arithmetic is interpreted via \({}\omega\) with ordinal addition and multiplication.

The first step is to turn syntax into arithmetic. Fix an effective coding of symbols, formulas, and finite sequences of formulas by natural numbers. Write \(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner\) for the code (Gödel number) of \({}\varphi\), and also for the numeral representing it in the language of arithmetic. Inside \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), one can just as well code syntax by hereditarily finite sets.

Since \({}T\) is recursively axiomatized, the relation

\[ \operatorname{Prf}_T(p, x) \; :\iff \; p \text{ codes a $T$-proof of the formula coded by } x \]

is decidable: one only needs to check that every step of the finite sequence coded by \(p\) is an axiom or follows from earlier steps by a rule of inference. Decidable relations can be expressed in arithmetic by formulas with only bounded quantifiers \(\forall y < t\), \(\exists y < t\) (more precisely, by formulas that \({}T\) proves equivalent to such formulas on all numerals). We call such formulas \(\Delta^0_0\). Note the analogy with the set-theoretic \(\Delta_0\) formulas from Models of Set Theory.

A formula is \(\Sigma^0_1\) if it has the form \(\exists y\, \theta\) with \(\theta\) in \(\Delta^0_0\), and \(\Pi^0_1\) if it has the form \(\forall y\, \theta\) with \(\theta\) in \(\Delta^0_0\).

Definition 1
The provability predicate of \({}T\) is the \(\Sigma^0_1\) formula \[ \operatorname{Prov}_T(x) \;:\equiv\; \exists p \; \operatorname{Prf}_T(p, x), \] and the consistency statement of \({}T\) is the \(\Pi^0_1\) sentence \[ \operatorname{Con}(T) \;:\equiv\; \neg\, \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner 0 = 1 \urcorner). \]

\(\operatorname{Con}(T)\) is a sentence of arithmetic. It says “no natural number codes a \(T\)-proof of \(0 = 1\).” It is a statement about \(\mathbb{N}\) and nothing more, and it can be checked instance by instance: each single number either codes such a proof or it does not.

Observe the two levels at work. “\({}T\) is consistent” is a statement of the metatheory. \(\operatorname{Con}(T)\) is its formal shadow, a sentence that \({}T\) itself can attempt to prove.

The key bridge between true arithmetic facts and provability is the following.

Lemma 1 (\(\Sigma^0_1\)-completeness)
If \({}\sigma\) is a \(\Sigma^0_1\) sentence that is true in \(\mathbb{N}\), then \(\mathsf{Q}\vdash \sigma\), and hence \(T \vdash \sigma\).

In particular, if \(T \vdash \varphi\), then the statement \(\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)\) is a true \(\Sigma^0_1\) sentence (a proof exists), so \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)\).

The diagonal lemma

The second ingredient is a way to construct self-referential sentences.

Lemma 2 (Diagonal lemma)
For every formula \(\psi(x)\) with one free variable, there is a sentence \({}\sigma\) such that \[ T \vdash \sigma \leftrightarrow \psi(\ulcorner \sigma \urcorner). \]

The first incompleteness theorem

Apply Lemma 2 to \(\psi(x) :\equiv \neg \operatorname{Prov}_T(x)\). We obtain a sentence \({}G\), the Gödel sentence of \({}T\), such that

\[ T \vdash G \leftrightarrow \neg \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner). \]

Informally, \({}G\) says “I am not provable in \({}T\).”

Theorem 1 (Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem)
If \({}T\) is consistent, then \(T \nvdash G\). If \({}T\) is moreover \(\omega\)-consistent, then also \(T \nvdash \neg G\).

NoteProof

Proof. Suppose \(T \vdash G\). By \(\Sigma^0_1\)-completeness, \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)\). But from \(G\) and the defining property of \({}G\), also \(T \vdash \neg \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)\). Hence \({}T\) is inconsistent.

Now suppose \(T \vdash \neg G\), so \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)\), that is, \(T \vdash \exists p\, \operatorname{Prf}_T(p, \ulcorner G \urcorner)\). By the first part, \(T \nvdash G\), so no number \(n\) codes a proof of \({}G\), and by \(\Sigma^0_1\)-completeness \(T \vdash \neg \operatorname{Prf}_T(\underline{n}, \ulcorner G \urcorner)\) for every \(n\). This is exactly what \(\omega\)-consistency rules out.

The assumption of \(\omega\)-consistency can be weakened to plain consistency by a cleverer choice of self-referential sentence (Rosser’s trick). We will not need this.

Corollary 1
If \({}T\) is consistent, then \({}G\) is true in \(\mathbb{N}\) but not provable in \({}T\).

NoteProof

Proof. \({}G\) is equivalent to \(\neg \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G \urcorner)\), which states exactly what Theorem 1 established.

The second incompleteness theorem

The proof of the first part of Theorem 1 was finitistic: it used nothing but elementary reasoning about finite syntactic objects. By the thesis that every finitistic argument can be formalized, \({}T\) should be able to carry it out internally. Making this precise is laborious, and the standard route goes through three properties of \(\operatorname{Prov}_T\), the Hilbert–Bernays–Löb derivability conditions. For all sentences \({}\varphi, \psi\):

  • (D1) If \(T \vdash \varphi\), then \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)\).
  • (D2) \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi\to \psi \urcorner) \to \big(\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \to \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \psi \urcorner)\big)\).
  • (D3) \(T \vdash \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \to \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \urcorner)\).

(D1) is Lemma 1. (D2) formalizes modus ponens. (D3) is (D1) formalized inside \({}T\): it is a formalized version of \(\Sigma^0_1\)-completeness, and its verification is where the real work lies. We take (D1)–(D3) for granted.

Theorem 2 (Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem)
If \({}T\) is consistent, then \(T \nvdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\).

NoteProof

Proof. We show \(T \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T) \to G\). Then \(T \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\) would imply \(T \vdash G\), contradicting Theorem 1.

Write \(P(\varphi)\) for \(\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)\). Since \(T \vdash G \to \neg P(G)\), (D1) and (D2) give \[ T \vdash P(G) \to P(\neg P(G)). \] By (D3), \(T \vdash P(G) \to P(P(G))\). Using (D1) and (D2) once more, applied to the tautology \(P(G) \to (\neg P(G) \to 0 = 1)\), we can combine the two: \[ T \vdash P(G) \to P(0 = 1). \] Contraposing, \(T \vdash \neg P(0=1) \to \neg P(G)\), that is, \(T \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T) \to \neg P(G)\). Since \(T \vdash \neg P(G) \to G\), we obtain \(T \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T) \to G\).

This settles Hilbert’s program in the negative: finitistic metamathematics can be formalized in \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), so if it could establish the consistency of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), then \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) would prove \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\).

Consistency and models

For set theory, the most useful form of \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\) is semantic. In Models of Set Theory we used Gödel’s completeness theorem in the metatheory: if \(\mathsf{ZF}\) is consistent, then it has a model. Both the soundness and completeness theorems are themselves theorems of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), when the satisfaction relation \(M \models \varphi\) for set-sized structures \(M\) is defined inside \(\mathsf{ZFC}\). This gives:

Proposition 1
\[ \mathsf{ZFC}\vdash \; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \leftrightarrow \exists M\, (M \models \mathsf{ZFC}). \]

Combining this with Theorem 2:

Corollary 2
If \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) is consistent, then \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) does not prove that there exists a set model of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\). In particular, \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) does not prove the existence of a transitive model of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), or of an ordinal \({}\alpha\) with \(V_\alpha \models \mathsf{ZFC}\).

Inaccessible cardinals and consistency

Let \(\mathsf{IC}\) denote the axiom “there exists a (strongly) inaccessible cardinal.” Recall from Large Cardinals that if \({}\kappa\) is inaccessible, then \(V_\kappa \models \mathsf{ZFC}\). With Proposition 1, this yields:

Proposition 2
\[ \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} \;\vdash\; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}). \]

NoteProof

Proof. Argue in \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\). Let \({}\kappa\) be inaccessible. Then \(V_\kappa\) is a set and \(V_\kappa \models \mathsf{ZFC}\), so \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\) holds by Proposition 1.

Corollary 3
If \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) is consistent, then \(\mathsf{ZFC}\nvdash \mathsf{IC}\).

NoteProof

Proof. If \(\mathsf{ZFC}\vdash \mathsf{IC}\), then \(\mathsf{ZFC}\vdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\) by Proposition 2, contradicting Theorem 2.

We had seen a second proof of Corollary 3 in Large Cardinals: if \({}\kappa\) is the least inaccessible, then \(V_\kappa \models \mathsf{ZFC}+ \neg\mathsf{IC}\), since being inaccessible is absolute for \(V_\kappa\). Formalized, this argument shows

\[ \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} \;\vdash\; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \neg \mathsf{IC}). \]

So the absoluteness argument tells us that \(\neg\mathsf{IC}\) is consistent relative to \(\mathsf{IC}\). The second incompleteness theorem tells us much more: we cannot even show that \(\mathsf{IC}\) is consistent relative to \(\mathsf{ZFC}\).

Theorem 3
If \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) is consistent, then \[ \mathsf{ZFC}\nvdash \; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \to \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}). \]

NoteProof

Proof. Suppose \(\mathsf{ZFC}\vdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \to \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC})\). Then the same implication is provable in the stronger theory \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\). By Proposition 2, \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} \vdash \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\). Combining the two, \[ \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} \;\vdash\; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}), \] and by Theorem 2, \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) is inconsistent.

In other words, the existence of an inaccessible cardinal is not merely unprovable in \(\mathsf{ZFC}\). It is a genuinely stronger assumption: accepting it commits us to new arithmetic truths, such as \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC})\), that \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) cannot reach.

Two kinds of independence

Compare this with the Continuum Hypothesis. By Gödel’s constructible universe \({}L\) and Cohen’s method of forcing (which we will study later),

\[ \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}) \iff \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{CH}) \iff \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \neg\mathsf{CH}), \]

and these equivalences are provable in a weak base theory. So both \(\mathsf{CH}\) and \(\mathsf{IC}\) are independent of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), but in different ways.

  • \(\mathsf{CH}\) is equiconsistent independent: adding \(\mathsf{CH}\) or its negation costs nothing in terms of consistency.
  • \(\mathsf{IC}\) is strength-increasing independent: adding it proves new \(\Pi^0_1\) sentences.

Consistency strength

The phenomenon of the previous section can be organized into an ordering of theories. We fix a weak base theory \({}B\), say \(\mathsf{PA}\), or \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) when we compare extensions of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), and consider recursively axiomatized theories extending \({}B\).

Definition 2 (Consistency strength)
Let \({}T\) and \({}S\) be theories. Define \[ T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S \;:\iff\; B \vdash \operatorname{Con}(S) \to \operatorname{Con}(T). \] We say \({}T\) and \({}S\) are equiconsistent, \(T \equiv_{\operatorname{Con}} S\), if \(T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) and \(S \le_{\operatorname{Con}} T\). We write \(T <_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) if \(T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S\) and \(S \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\).

The relation \(\le_{\operatorname{Con}}\) is reflexive and transitive. The notation \(<_{\operatorname{Con}}\) is justified by the following generalization of Theorem 3.

Proposition 3
Suppose \({}S\) is consistent and \(S \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\). Then \(S \not\le_{\operatorname{Con}} T\).

NoteProof

Proof. Suppose \(B \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T) \to \operatorname{Con}(S)\). Since \({}S\) extends \({}B\) and \(S \vdash \operatorname{Con}(T)\), we get \(S \vdash \operatorname{Con}(S)\), contradicting Theorem 2.

Example 1
The following relations hold.

Relation Method
\(\mathsf{ZF}\equiv_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}\equiv_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{GCH}\) the inner model \({}L\) Gödel 1938
\(\mathsf{ZFC}\equiv_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}+ \neg\mathsf{CH}\) forcing Cohen 1963
\(\mathsf{PA} <_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}\) \({}\omega\) is a model of \(\mathsf{PA}\)
\(\mathsf{ZFC}<_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) \(V_\kappa \models \mathsf{ZFC}\) Proposition 2
\(\mathsf{ZF}+ \mathsf{DC}+ \text{``every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable''} \equiv_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) forcing / the inner model \({}L\) Solovay 1970, Shelah 1984

The last line connects to the measure problem: in \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) there are non-measurable sets of reals, but if we drop full Choice and keep only Dependent Choice, “every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable” is consistent, at exactly the cost of an inaccessible cardinal.

Upper and lower bounds

How does one establish such equiconsistencies? There are two directions.

  • Upper bounds: build a model. To show \(T \le_{\operatorname{Con}} S\), one assumes a model of \({}S\) and builds from it a model of \({}T\). The models \(V_\kappa\) and forcing extensions are of this kind. Solovay’s half of the last example starts with an inaccessible and builds, by forcing, a model of \(\mathsf{ZF}+ \mathsf{DC}\) in which every set of reals is measurable.
  • Lower bounds: find the large cardinal in an inner model. To show \(S \le_{\operatorname{Con}} T\), one assumes \({}T\) and shows that some inner model, such as \({}L\), satisfies \({}S\). Shelah’s half of the example shows that if all (\(\Sigma^1_3\)) sets of reals are measurable, then \(\omega_1\) is an inaccessible cardinal in \({}L\).

A remarkable empirical fact: all “natural” theories studied so far line up linearly under \(\le_{\operatorname{Con}}\), and large cardinal axioms provide a scale against which they can be measured. There is no known explanation for this phenomenon, and one can construct artificial theories (using self-reference) that are incomparable.

Weakly compact cardinals

To see that the consistency hierarchy continues above inaccessibles, we return to the partition properties from Large Cardinals. Recall that \(\kappa \to (\lambda)^2_2\) means that every partition \(F : [\kappa]^2 \to \{0, 1\}\) has a homogeneous set of size \({}\lambda\), and that Ramsey’s theorem gives \(\aleph_0 \to (\aleph_0)^2_2\).

Definition 3
An uncountable cardinal \({}\kappa\) is weakly compact if \(\kappa \to (\kappa)^2_2\).

Write \(\mathsf{WC}\) for the axiom “there exists a weakly compact cardinal.”

Weakly compact cardinals are inaccessible

Proposition 4
Every weakly compact cardinal is regular.

For the strong limit property, we compare well-orders with the lexicographic order on \(2^\lambda\), the set of functions \(f: \lambda \to \{0, 1\}\): \[ f <_{\mathop{\mathrm{lex}}} g \;:\iff\; f(\xi) < g(\xi), \text{ where $\xi$ is least with } f(\xi) \neq g(\xi). \] This is a linear order. Its key property is that it contains no long well-ordered sequences.

Lemma 3 (Sierpiński)
For every infinite cardinal \({}\lambda\), \((2^\lambda, <_{\mathop{\mathrm{lex}}})\) has no strictly increasing and no strictly decreasing sequence of length \(\lambda^+\).

Exercise 1 Prove Lemma 3.

Hint: Suppose \((f_\alpha)_{\alpha < \lambda^+}\) is strictly increasing. First let \(\gamma \leq \lambda\) be least such that the restrictions \(f_\alpha \mid_{\gamma}\) take \(\lambda^+\) many values, and pass to a subsequence to reduce to the case where all restrictions \(f_\alpha \mid_{\xi}\), \(\xi < \gamma\), take at most \({}\lambda\) values in total. Then let \(\xi_\alpha\) be least where \(f_\alpha\) and \(f_{\alpha+1}\) differ and show that \(\alpha \mapsto f_\alpha \mid_{\xi_\alpha}\) is injective.

Proposition 5
Every weakly compact cardinal is inaccessible.

Weakly compact cardinals are strictly stronger

Weakly compact cardinals are much larger than the first inaccessible.

Proposition 6
\[ \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{WC} \;\vdash\; \operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}), \] and hence, if \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{WC}\) is consistent, \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC} <_{\operatorname{Con}} \mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{WC}\).

The large cardinal hierarchy

We have climbed two rungs of a much longer ladder.

\[ \begin{align} \mathsf{ZFC}\;<_{\operatorname{Con}}\; \text{inaccessible} & \;<_{\operatorname{Con}}\; \text{weakly compact} \\ & \;<_{\operatorname{Con}}\; \text{Ramsey} \;<_{\operatorname{Con}}\; \text{measurable} \;<_{\operatorname{Con}}\; \cdots \end{align} \]

  • The rungs are genuinely separated. For example, if \({}\kappa\) is measurable, then the set of weakly compact cardinals below \({}\kappa\) belongs to every normal ultrafilter on \({}\kappa\), so \({}\kappa\) is the \(\kappa\)-th weakly compact cardinal.
  • The ladder continues far beyond measurables, through strong, Woodin, and supercompact cardinals, all the way up to axioms that assert the existence of nontrivial elementary embeddings \(j : V_\lambda \to V_\lambda\).
  • There is a ceiling. Kunen’s inconsistency theorem shows, in \(\mathsf{ZFC}\), that there is no nontrivial elementary embedding \(j : V \to V\). Large cardinal axioms are therefore not arbitrary: the most naive strengthening is outright inconsistent.

What does it mean to accept a large cardinal?

Theorem 3 shows that the consistency of \(\mathsf{ZFC}+ \mathsf{IC}\) can never be established on the basis of \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) alone. So why do set theorists regard large cardinal axioms as legitimate, or even as true? Several kinds of reasons are commonly offered.

  • Reflection and the iterative conception. The universe \(V\) is built in stages \(V_\alpha\), and it should be “too large” to be fully described from within. An inaccessible \({}\kappa\) is a stage \(V_\kappa\) that already looks like the whole universe as far as \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) can tell. Indescribable cardinals strengthen this: no property of a given complexity can distinguish \(V_\kappa\) from \(V\).
  • Fruitfulness and convergence. Large cardinals settle questions about sets of reals that \(\mathsf{ZFC}\) leaves open, and very different approaches lead to the same large cardinal hypotheses, as with the measurability example above.
  • Track record. Decades of intensive study have not produced a contradiction from the axioms below Kunen’s bound.

Whatever one makes of these, the incompleteness theorems give large cardinals a precise arithmetic meaning. Each rung of the ladder proves new \(\Pi^0_1\) theorems, namely the consistency of the rungs below it. In this sense, accepting a stronger large cardinal is a commitment about the natural numbers: that certain searches for contradictions will never succeed.
